What a chapter is for

We started the club at Mayo High School in October 2025. Our mission is to provide every student with access to a foundational AI education: to help students understand AI, evaluate its output, and preserve their own judgment. Chapters are how the program reaches other schools.

A chapter runs the club’s program at its own school, with lessons it can run as written. It meets once a month and runs one AI literacy event each semester. At Mayo we meet every Thursday, one topic talked through around a table.

A student founder

What we provide

Lessons you can run as written
Curriculum and lesson materials the chapter can run as written, so nobody has to build a program from scratch.
Support over video
Shyam Christensen and Adam Keegan will support each new chapter directly through its founding year. It happens over video, so it works the same in any state.
A vote on the Central Board
A seat on the Central Board, where every chapter president holds a vote in how the network is run.
In plain words: your chapter has a say in how the network is run, through its president’s vote.

What happens next

Putting your name in is interest, not approval. Before a chapter exists, three things have to be in place.

  1. A student founder

    It starts with the founder form, on Google Forms.

  2. A faculty advisor

    A faculty member at your school agrees to advise the chapter.

  3. Your school’s approval process

    It is your school’s own. Nothing goes ahead until it has been followed.

Nothing commits a school until a founder and an advisor are both in place and the school’s own approval process has been followed.
